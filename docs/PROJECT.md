## Problem and users
Resource-constrained SMEs may have technical and continuity documents but lack the specialist time and expertise to turn them into meaningful cyber exercises and review findings. The technical workstream must convert an SME's network and continuity information into a source-linked, human-confirmed profile and an organisation-specific exercise. It supports selected Cyber Trust Mark exercise and improvement requirements, particularly B.21.4/B.21.5; it does not certify compliance. The AI-assisted TTX Platform encompasses separate technical and operational exercise tracks and assists preparation, facilitated play and review. Use "TTX Platform" for the product; "technical" identifies an exercise track, not the whole platform. Users are a planner/profile reviewer, facilitator, participant teams and an exercise reviewer. A person may hold multiple functions, but participant access remains distinct from facilitator authority.

Audience clarification, 4 October 2026 (ADR-022): focus on SME organisations preparing for or operating at Cyber Trust tiers 3-5, rather than a foundational tiers 1-2 training course. Treat basic incident roles, reporting/escalation arrangements and an accessible response plan as expected starting context to confirm, not capabilities to invent during inject 1. These are organisation-level preparedness tiers, not individual participant qualifications; targeting them neither proves prior attainment nor adds certification screening to the prototype. The standards mapping is in [architecture](architecture.md#step-0---baseline-preparation).

### Step 0 - Baseline preparation

Before the first technical inject, record a short team contact-route check, then provide the role-appropriate briefing and facilitator confirmation of starting arrangements. This is ordinary preparation for Form B's role-based exercise, not a sixth inject, a scored CTM assessment or the deferred generic readiness engine. The operational worker must define their track's starting arrangements; the technical call-tree policy and wording are not inherited automatically (ADR-025).

- Establish who represents each responding function, the incident lead and escalation/cover arrangements, including the internal team, MSP and MSSP. Respondents must cover every participant role declared in the technical package, not only inject 1's recipients. Record unavailable or unassigned cover rather than inventing it.
- Ask the responding team whom they would contact first, how they would find/use the contact details, and their fallback/escalation route. The facilitator may record an oral answer; references may be consulted. Record respondents, all three answers, a Ready / Clarification required / Hold decision and its rationale before releasing inject 1. Do not prefill the team's answers or reveal the prepared contact answer first. Preserve earlier answers and corrections.
- Identify decision authority and available response references. Keep knowledge of a documented route separate from verified contactability or rehearsed capability. Clarification/Hold blocks play until a reviewed correction or explicit exercise assumption is recorded as Ready; this is not an automatic CTM pass/fail.
- Make the synthetic/simulation boundary explicit: no real calls, notifications, filings or system changes. Supply only the context appropriate to each role, not facilitator criteria, future injects or every specialist weakness.
- Record unresolved preparation gaps and their disposition. An essential missing arrangement requires clarification, an explicit reviewed synthetic exercise assumption, a narrower scope or a hold before play. Deliberate weaknesses may remain when they serve a reviewed objective; they are not silently repaired, scored as automatic failure or treated as evidence that an organisation passed or failed a CTM tier.

The existing organisation pack contains names and escalation information, but also a stale duty card, missing internal cover and unrehearsed arrangements. It is not a proven tier 3-5 organisation. ### Technical response policy

ADR-025 adopts these bounded defaults under the user's delegated project judgement. Use one team per run and one agreed response per released position, with append-only revisions. Any assigned participant can submit or revise it; all assigned participants see the latest team response, while the facilitator sees every revision and records a separate attributed disposition. Inject evidence remains recipient-only, and facilitator observations remain private. No facilitator-on-behalf response submission or coached attempt limit is introduced.

Pausing freezes new response, disposition, closure and completion writes as well as releases. Existing evidence remains readable, and unsent drafts stay in browser memory, not durable storage. Resume explicitly before further writes. Closing a position requires the current response's reviewed disposition, or an explicit unanswered finding; Hold and Clarification required are not closure. Close the current position before approving/releasing the next. Complete the run explicitly after every required position is closed; neither the last release nor completion proves successful incident recovery. The shared position/variant/per-role evidence contract still needs coordination before five-inject expansion.

### Exercise preparation and review flow

Prior expanded flow under ADR-016; not implemented and subject to ADR-021's Form B-first gate. Use methodology as design guidance, not as authority to add baseline deliverables. 1. Establish exercise purpose, one track, intended audience and initial scope before detailed exercise-specific intake. Refine objectives after reviewing the available context.
2. Ingest the agreed synthetic network-diagram format and any supplied supported text-based BCP/BIA/DRP. Extract candidate facts and relationships with source locators; use guided questions to complete missing context. 7. Draft the AAR from the recorded evidence, review it with a human, and assign improvement actions with owners and due dates. Action-closure tracking is deferred. Separate response/plan/capability findings from exercise-design limitations.

## Terminology and source precedence
MSEL means Master Scenario Events List. An inject is versioned information for defined recipients. Package approval accepts a definition and its branches; release approval permits specific content and recipients in a run state.
